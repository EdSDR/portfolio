This is a recreation of the hypergraph in Torus Portal, the app people use to explore and manage
the Torus network. I built the original at Renlabs in 2025. The scene above runs on generated data,
but it uses the same library, shapes and colors as the real one.

## The original

Torus is a Substrate blockchain for networks of autonomous agents. A root allocator pays emissions
to agents, and agents can delegate emissions or capabilities to other agents through permissions.
Delegations can chain, so the structure gets deep quickly and a table stops helping.

The Portal draws the network as a 3D force-directed graph. The allocator is pinned at the center.
Agents are spheres, colored by whether they're whitelisted. Signals are tetrahedrons. Permissions
get their own small icosahedrons instead of being plain edges, because one permission can connect a
delegator to several recipients and distribution targets. That's what makes it a hypergraph, and
where the name comes from. Particles run along the links, more of them on heavier weights.

## My part

I started the graph in May 2025 and wrote most of it: the 3D view, the click-to-focus camera, flow
highlighting, the side sheet with an agent's permissions, and a 2D version in PixiJS. Other people
on the team worked on it too; Rodrigo reworked the search, for example.

## How it works

The graph uses r3f-forcegraph, a React Three Fiber wrapper around three-forcegraph and
d3-force-3d. A worker indexes permissions, agents and signals from the chain into Postgres on every
block, and the page reads them through four tRPC queries. The client turns rows into nodes and
links, seeds agents on a ring so the simulation starts from a reasonable shape, and sets the
forces (link distance 130, charge −60). Bloom from `@react-three/postprocessing` makes the nodes
glow.

Clicking a node flies the camera to it with a small hand-written ease-in-out. The flight stops once
it's within 5 units or 90% of the way there, because the last few percent of an ease just crawl.
The selection lives in the URL (`?id=`), so a shared link opens the graph focused on that agent.

## The tricky bit

Flow highlighting: pick an agent and dim everything it isn't connected to. The obvious version
walks links recursively, and it selected the whole graph every time, because every agent connects
to the allocator. The fix was to walk everything except the allocator and add it back at the end.
The dimming itself is only material opacity, 0.1 to 0.2 on the nodes outside the flow.

## About this recreation

There's no chain here. The graph comes from a seeded random generator: 16 root clusters, about 600
nodes and 690 links, with the production palette and shapes. Cards on this site don't take input,
so there's no camera focus or selection. The scene gets its own canvas with Bloom at 4× MSAA,
pauses when the card scrolls out of view, and shows a pre-rendered poster until its first frame
is ready.

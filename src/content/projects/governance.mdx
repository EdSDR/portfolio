This recreates the landing scene of the Torus governance app, which I built in April 2025. Before
you get to proposals and votes, you see a statue of Themis, the Greek figure of justice, lit by one
spotlight in fog and starlight.

## The original

The governance app is where TORUS holders run the DAO: proposals (including emission changes and
treasury transfers), voting, applications from agents that want to be whitelisted, a dashboard of
agent health, and DAO membership tied to a Discord account. Chain data comes in through the
polkadot.js SDK; comments and off-chain votes go through tRPC and Drizzle.

The landing page used to be a static image of a Greek statue. I replaced it with a 3D version: a
glTF model of Themis, fog, volumetric clouds, stars, and a spotlight that follows the mouse. Three
buttons float inside the scene and lead to proposals, the DAO dashboard and whitelist applications.

## My part

The scene was mine, start to finish, in one PR. I also worked on the rest of the governance app
with the team.

## How it works

The statue is a single Draco-compressed mesh, 2.9 MB, drawn with a plain Lambert material. The
look comes from lighting and fog, not textures. The pointer drives the motion through maath's
damping helpers: the statue turns toward the cursor and the spotlight tracks the pointer across
the front. Soft shadows came from drei's `SoftShadows` (PCSS); the clouds and stars are drei too.

## The tricky bit

Bringing it into this site broke two things.

First, the statue vanished. `SoftShadows` injects shader code that three.js 0.186 no longer
supports, so the statue's material failed to compile and the mesh quietly stopped drawing, while
the clouds and stars kept rendering. I switched to variance shadow maps (VSM).

Second, the model is 772k triangles with no shared vertices (every corner carries its own normal),
about 65 MB once decoded. I dropped the normals, welded vertices by position, simplified it with
meshoptimizer, and recomputed smooth normals: 193k triangles, 590 KB. At 10% of the original the
thin chains on the scales broke apart. 25% kept them.

## About this recreation

Cards here don't take pointer input, so the parallax became a slow automatic sway and the spotlight
drifts on its own. The key light sits off to one side, so the shadows are harder than in the
original. The cloud texture is served from this site instead of a CDN.
